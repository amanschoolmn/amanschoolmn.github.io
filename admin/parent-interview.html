02 — Placement

Three Levels.
Skill-Based, Not Age-Based.

🌱
Level 1 — Beginner مُبْتَدِئ
Green Book · مُعَلِّمُ الْقِرَاءَةِ الْعَرَبِيَّةِ مَعَ قَاعِدَةٍ بَغْدَادِيَّة
Arabic alphabet, Qur'an reading foundations, basic supplications & Islamic identity.
📖
Level 2 — Intermediate مُتَوَسِّط
Qur'an Reading · Juz ʿAmma · Juz Tabārak · Juz Qad Samiʿ
Reading from the Muṣḥaf, building fluency & accuracy until the tongue flows easily and correctly.
⭐
Level 3 — Advanced مُتَقَدِّم
Ḥifẓ Program · Full Qur'an Memorization
Multiple juz memorized and reviewed regularly with full tajwīd & leadership responsibilities.

Your child will be assessed and placed at the level that is right for them. Advancement happens when they are truly ready — not by age or calendar.

Speaker Notes
Explain the placement process: every new student is assessed by you personally before their first class. Be honest: some parents expect their child to be placed at a higher level than they are ready for. Address this directly: 'We place children where they will thrive — not where it feels good on paper. A child placed in the wrong level doesn't grow. Our placement is always in the best interest of the child.' Mention Level 2 specifically — some parents won't understand why their child isn't memorizing yet. Explain the tongue needs time to be ready for Ḥifẓ.

03 — The Parent Covenant

Your Role Is Essential

AMAN is not a drop-off service. We are partners. What happens at home determines whether what happens in class takes root.

📖
Practice Qur'an daily
Sit with your child and have them recite what they learned. Even 10 minutes.
📋
Review homework weekly
Check their notebook. Know what they are studying. Ask them questions.
🏠
Reinforce Islamic manners at home
What we build in class must be lived at home. Adab starts with you.
📞
Communicate with the teacher
If something is wrong at home — tell us. We can't support what we don't know about.
💳
Pay fees on time
$90/month per student. Due at the start of each month — no exceptions.
⏰
Prioritize attendance
This is not optional weekend enrichment. This is their deen. Treat it that way.
Speaker Notes
This is the most important slide for PARENTS. Look at them directly and say: 'I can be the best teacher in the world — but if the learning stops when your child walks out the door on Sunday, it won't stick. You are the most important teacher your child has. I am here to support you, not replace you.' The point about Qur'an practice at home is critical — students who practice with parents at home advance 3x faster. Make that real for them.

04 — Our Standards

Non-Negotiables

These are not arbitrary rules. Each one protects the learning environment and honors the Islamic character of this school.

⏰
Punctuality
15+ minutes late = sent home. The opening adhkār sets the tone for the entire day. Every child deserves to be present for it.
📅
Attendance
More than 3 unexcused absences may result in dismissal. Notify us by Friday for any planned absence.
🧕
Islamic Dress
Proper hijab for sisters. Thobe or appropriate Islamic dress for brothers. Mandatory every session. Non-compliance = not admitted that day.
🚫
Conduct
Disrespect toward teachers or classmates is not tolerated. Boys and girls remain separated at all times per Islamic guidelines.
📵
No Devices
Phones and personal devices are not permitted during class. They undermine focus and violate the sacred environment we maintain.
⚠️
Grounds for Removal
Consistent disrespect, repeated violations, or failure to meet the parent covenant may result in dismissal — with or without warning.

Also expected: Qur'an, notebook & pencil brought every week, homework submitted on time, and food/drinks kept to the designated break — plus on-time pickup at the end of each session.

Speaker Notes
Be clear and calm here — don't apologize for the standards. A powerful thing to say: 'These rules are not about being strict for its own sake. They are about protecting what we have built. One child who consistently disrupts affects 35 others. We protect the many.' On the dress code — be direct: if a child shows up without proper Islamic dress, they go home. No exceptions. On removal: 'We do not dismiss students lightly. But we will, if necessary. Your signature on the parent agreement means you accept this.' Don't soften it.

This conversation goes both ways. We want to know about your child — their personality, what they love, what's been hard for them, what you hope for them.

What is your child like at home?

Speaker Notes
This is your longest slide in terms of time. Put the presentation aside and just talk. Ask the questions on the screen genuinely. Listen. What they share here tells you whether this will work. Red flags: parents who have no interest in reinforcing at home, parents who have unrealistic expectations about their child's level, parents who seem resentful of the rules. Green flags: parents who are humble, who ask what they can do to help, who talk about their child with love and honesty. Trust your gut here.

If you sense this isn't going to work — the parent is dismissive of the rules, the child has serious behavioral issues they haven't disclosed, the family isn't committed — it is better for everyone to say so honestly. Frame it not as rejection but as protection: 'I want to make sure every child in my class can thrive. Sometimes a different environment is actually the better answer for a particular child right now.'
