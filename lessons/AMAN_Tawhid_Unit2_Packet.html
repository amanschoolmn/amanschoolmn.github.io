Q1 & Q2 — Who Is Your Lord? Meaning of Rabb

Slide ReferenceDeck A: Slides 4–9 (Q1&2, Rabb Deep-Dive) · Deck B: Lesson 1 (Q1/Q2 slides)

MaterialsPrinted packet, pencils, board/whiteboard, index cards (flashcard drill)

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 2, students will be able to:

  • Recite Q1 (مَنْ رَبُّكَ) and its answer (رَبِّي اللَّهُ) instantly, from memory. (Recall)
  • Recite Q2 (مَا مَعْنَى الرَّبِّ) and its answer (الْمَالِكُ الْمَعْبُودُ) from memory. (Recall)
  • Explain in their own words what "al-Mālik" (The Owner) and "al-Maʿbūd" (The One Worshipped) mean. (Comprehension)
  • State the meaning of the Name "Allah" — ذُو الْأُلُوهِيَّةِ وَالْعُبُودِيَّةِ عَلَى خَلْقِهِ أَجْمَعِينَ. (Recall)
  • Distinguish Q1 (identifying WHO your Lord is) from Q2 (explaining WHAT that title means). (Comprehension)
  • Apply the meaning of Rabb to explain why only Allah deserves worship. (Application)

Weekend 3 · Session 5 (Saturday) — Q1: Who Is Your Lord?

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "If someone stopped you on the street and asked 'who is in charge of you?' — what would you say? Who is REALLY in charge of everything?"

Guided Classroom Activity 25 min

Teacher instructions: This is the FIRST of the book's six grave-questions. From today on, drill it exactly like a real Q&A — ask the question, students answer as a class, then individually.

[س١]: إِذَا قِيلَ لَكَ: مَنْ رَبُّكَ؟
Question 1: "If it is said to you: Who is your Lord?"
[جـ]: فَقُلْ: رَبِّي اللَّهُ
Answer: "Then say: My Lord is Allah."
Teacher script

Ask 3–4 students cold-call style: "Man rabbuka?" — expect the full answer "Rabbī Allāh," not just "Allah." Point out: we don't just name Allah, we say "MY Lord" — it's personal, not just a fact we know about the world.

Explain: this exact question will be asked to every person in the grave. This is why the book starts here — this is the single most important question a person will ever answer.

Discussion prompt: Why do you think the answer starts with "My" (رَبِّي) instead of just saying "Allah is the Lord"?

Exit Ticket 5 min

Have students answer on scrap paper:

Write Q1 and its answer, in Arabic if you can, or in English if not yet.

Weekend 3 · Session 6 (Sunday) — Q2: The Meaning of Rabb

Bell Ringer 5 min

Cold-call 3–4 students: "Man rabbuka?" — expect instant "Rabbī Allāh" with no hesitation.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

[س٢]: وَمَا مَعْنَى «الرَّبِّ»؟
Question 2: "And what is the meaning of ar-Rabb?"
[جـ]: فَقُلْ: الْمَالِكُ الْمَعْبُودُ
Answer: "Then say: The Owner, the One Worshipped."
Teacher script

Al-Mālik (المالك) — the Owner. Ask: "If I own something, what can I do with it that you can't?" (decide what happens to it). Allah truly owns everything that exists — not just controls it, OWNS it.

Al-Maʿbūd (المعبود) — the One Worshipped. This means Allah is the ONLY one who deserves worship, because He is the Mālik. Ownership comes first, worship follows — you worship Him BECAUSE He is truly your Owner and Sustainer.

The Meaning of the Name "Allah"

وَمَعْنَى «اللهُ»: ذُو الْأُلُوهِيَّةِ وَالْعُبُودِيَّةِ عَلَى خَلْقِهِ أَجْمَعِينَ
"And the meaning of 'Allah': the Possessor of the right to be worshipped, and of servitude, over all of His creation."

Teacher script: Point out this is a SEPARATE statement from Q2's answer — it explains the Name "Allah" itself, not the word "Rabb." Every single created thing — even a leaf falling, even a rock — is in a state of ʿubūdiyyah (servitude) to Allah, whether it "chooses" to or not. Humans are the only creation that can CHOOSE to worship Him or refuse.

A note for you, the teacher

Some English translations of this book give a longer answer to Q2 — six terms instead of two (adding "the One who aids and supports, the Creator and Sustainer"). Both are legitimate; this packet uses the book's shorter two-term Arabic answer (المالك المعبود) as the word-for-word memorization line, and covers the fuller list separately below as enrichment. Pick whichever your madrasah has standardized on.

Independent Practice 10 min

1. Fill in the blank: The meaning of ar-Rabb is: al-Mālik al-______________.

2. True or False: Al-Mālik means "The Owner."

3. In your own words, why does being the "Owner" mean Allah deserves to be worshipped?

Partner Activity — Q&A Flashcard Drill 10 min

Teacher instructions

Each pair gets 2 index cards: one with "س١" written on it, one with "س٢". Partner A holds up a card; Partner B must answer instantly in Arabic, then explain the meaning in English. Switch after each round. Repeat 3 rounds per pair for fluency.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize Q1 and Q2 with their answers, in Arabic, cold — no notes.
  • Ask a parent: "Can you think of something you own? What can you do with it that a stranger can't?" Write their answer, then explain how that connects to Allah being al-Mālik.

Weekend 4 · Session 7 (Saturday) — Deep Dive & Practice

Bell Ringer 5 min

Rapid recall (whole class, choral): Teacher calls "Q1!" — class answers together. Teacher calls "Q2!" — class answers together. Repeat 3x, increasing speed.

Enrichment: The Fuller Description of Rabb 15 min

الْمَالِكُ الْمَعْبُودُ، الْمُعِينُ وَالنَّاصِرُ، الْخَالِقُ وَالرَّازِقُ
"The Owner, the One Worshipped, the Helper and the Giver of Victory, the Creator and the Provider."

Teacher script: These six descriptions all describe the same Rabb from Q2 — they're not a different answer, just a fuller picture. Go through each briefly with an example students can relate to: al-Muʿīn (helps you when you're stuck), an-Nāṣir (gives victory/support), al-Khāliq (created everything from nothing), ar-Rāziq (provides your food, family, everything you have).

Group Activity — "Who Am I?" Riddle Game 15 min

How to play

Split into small groups. Read a riddle; the first group to shout the correct Arabic term wins the round:

  • "I am the one who truly owns everything that exists — who am I?" → الْمَالِك
  • "I am the only one worthy of worship — who am I?" → الْمَعْبُود
  • "Every creature bows to me, whether it wants to or not — what is this called?" → الْعُبُودِيَّة

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. Because Allah truly owns and sustains everything that exists, and only the true Owner and Sustainer of something deserves to be worshipped and obeyed by it.

2. Q1 asks WHO your Lord is (the identity — Allah), while Q2 asks WHAT that title "Rabb" actually means (the Owner, the One Worshipped).

Section F — Recite From Memory

مَنْ رَبُّكَ؟ رَبِّي اللَّهُ. مَا مَعْنَى الرَّبِّ؟ الْمَالِكُ الْمَعْبُودُ

Grading note: award 3/5 if only one of the two Q&A pairs is fully correct.

Section G — Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

The author added "all" to make clear that absolutely nothing is excluded — not one creature, not one atom is outside Allah's ownership and worship-worthiness. This rules out the idea that anything else could share in being worshipped, even partly.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

Your friend is missing the connection between al-Mālik and al-Maʿbūd — believing Allah created the world means admitting He's the true Owner (al-Mālik), and the book teaches that whoever is the true Owner is also the only one who deserves worship (al-Maʿbūd). You can't fully accept one without the other.
