Q6 — Who Is Your Prophet?

Teacher Overview

Slide ReferenceDeck A: Slide 25 (Q6) · Deck B: Lesson 5 (Q6 slides)

A note for you, the teacher

This begins the book's final major section — from here through the end, everything builds out the answer to Q6 (the Prophet ﷺ, his life, his companions, and the pillars of faith). Treat Q6 as the "hub" the rest of the year hangs off of.

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 10, students will be able to:

  • Recite Q6 and its full answer, including the Prophet's ﷺ complete lineage, from memory. (Recall)
  • Explain why the Prophet ﷺ was chosen from Quraysh specifically. (Comprehension)
  • Name examples of what the Arabs worshipped besides Allah before Islam. (Recall)
  • Describe the Prophet's ﷺ three-part mission in their own words. (Comprehension)
  • Explain what "sent to the red and the black" means. (Comprehension)

Weekend 20 · Session 39 (Saturday) — Q6: The Full Lineage

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "Do you know your own family lineage — your grandfather's name, or great-grandfather's? Why do you think people used to memorize their family lines so carefully?"

Spiral Review 5 min

Cold-call: "List the five pillars in order" — from Unit 9.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

[س٦]: وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَكَ: مَنْ نَبِيُّكَ؟
Question 6: "And if it is said to you: Who is your Prophet?"
[جـ]: فَقُلْ: نَبِيُّنَا مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ اللهِ بْنِ عَبْدِ الْمُطَّلِبِ بْنِ هَاشِمِ بْنِ عَبْدِ مَنَافٍ
Answer: "Then say: Our Prophet is Muḥammad, son of ʿAbdullāh, son of ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, son of Hāshim, son of ʿAbd Manāf."
مُحَمَّد بْنُ عَبْد اللهِ بْنِ عَبْد الْمُطَّلِب بْنِ هَاشِم بْنِ عَبْد مَنَاف
Teacher script

Have students repeat the chain link by link, then all together, several times, until it flows smoothly. This is a genuinely long answer to memorize — go slowly and don't rush past this session.

Exit Ticket 5 min

On scrap paper:

Write as much of the Prophet's ﷺ lineage as you can recall.

Weekend 20 · Session 40 (Sunday) — Chosen from Quraysh

Bell Ringer 5 min

Quick recall: Recite Q6's full answer together as a class, chain and all.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

اصْطَفَاهُ اللهُ تَعَالَى مِنْ قُرَيْشٍ، وَهُمْ صَفْوَةُ وَلَدِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ، وَبَعَثَهُ إِلَى الْأَحْمَرِ وَالْأَسْوَدِ
"Allah, the Most High, chose him from Quraysh — who are the best of the descendants of Ismāʿīl — and sent him to the red and the black (all of mankind)."
Teacher script

Explain "al-aḥmar wa'l-aswad" (the red and the black) simply: this is an old Arabic expression meaning EVERY people, of every description — the Prophet ﷺ wasn't sent to just one tribe or nation, but to all of humanity. Ask: "How is this different from earlier prophets, who were often sent to just their own people?"

Teacher note: also mention Hāshim was from Quraysh, Quraysh were Arabs, and the Arabs descend from Ismāʿīl, son of Ibrāhīm — connecting this unit back to Unit 8's mention of Ibrāhīm's declaration.

Independent Practice 10 min

1. Who did Allah choose the Prophet ﷺ from?

2. What does "sent to the red and the black" mean?

Partner Activity 10 min

Teacher instructions

Partner A tests Partner B on the full lineage chain, link by link, correcting any errors. Switch. Then discuss: "Why do you think it mattered to the Companions that the Prophet's ﷺ lineage could be traced this precisely?"

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize the full Q6 answer (all five names in the chain) and "chosen from Quraysh... sent to the red and the black."

Weekend 21 · Session 41 (Saturday) — His Mission

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "What do you think people worshipped before Islam came? Guess a few things."

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

وَأَنْزَلَ عَلَيْهِ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ، فَدَعَا النَّاسَ إِلَى إِخْلَاصِ الْعِبَادَةِ لِلَّهِ، وَتَرْكِ مَا كَانُوا يَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللهِ
"And He revealed to him the Book and wisdom, so he called people to sincerity of worship for Allah, and to leave what they used to worship besides Allah."

Before Islam, the Arabs worshipped many things besides Allah:

1
Idols (الأَصْنَام)
2
Stones (الأَحْجَار)
3
Trees (الأَشْجَار)
4
Prophets and righteous people (الأَنْبِيَاء وَالصَّالِحِين)
5
Angels, and others (الْمَلَائِكَة وَغَيْرِهِم)
Teacher script

Point out something important: several of these — prophets, righteous people, angels — are themselves GOOD in origin. Ask: "How can worshipping something good still be Shirk?" Connect back to Unit 5: it's not about whether the thing itself is good or bad, it's about whether worship is given to it instead of Allah.

The Three-Part Mission

فَدَعَا النَّاسَ إِلَى تَرْكِ الشِّرْكِ، وَقَاتَلَهُمْ إِلَى تَرْكِهِ، وَأَنْ يُخْلِصُوا الْعِبَادَةَ لِلَّهِ
"So he called people to leave Shirk, fought them until they left it, and called them to make worship sincerely for Allah."

Independent Practice 10 min

1. Name two things the Arabs worshipped besides Allah before Islam.

2. List the three parts of the Prophet's ﷺ mission.

Group Activity — Lineage Chain Relay 10 min

How to play

Teams line up; each student must add the next name in the chain before passing to the next teammate. Fastest correct team wins. Repeat with the three-part mission.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize what the Arabs worshipped before Islam and the three-part mission.

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. To show his lineage was known and verifiable, connecting him clearly to Quraysh, Ismāʿīl, and Ibrāhīm — this wasn't a claim made in secret or without proof.

2. Because Shirk isn't about whether the thing itself is good or bad — it's about whether worship is directed to it instead of Allah. Even something good, like an angel, doesn't deserve worship, because only Allah does.

Section F — Recite From Memory

مَنْ نَبِيُّكَ؟ نَبِيُّنَا مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ اللهِ بْنِ عَبْدِ الْمُطَّلِبِ بْنِ هَاشِمِ بْنِ عَبْدِ مَنَافٍ

Grading note: award 1 pt per correctly-recalled name in the chain if the full answer isn't complete.

Section G — Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

It tells us his message was universal, not limited to one nation or tribe like some earlier prophets — he was the final messenger sent for all of humanity, everywhere, for all time, not just for the Arabs of his own era.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

I'd explain that being good doesn't mean something deserves worship — angels are good and obedient to Allah, but worship belongs only to Allah Himself, the One who created the angels. Giving worship to anyone or anything else, even something good, takes something that only belongs to Allah.

Grading note for Sections E, G, H: use the Written Assignment Rubric — award full credit for the same idea in the student's own words.
