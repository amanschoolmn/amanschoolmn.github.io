Q3 — How Do You Know Your Lord?

Teacher Overview

Weekend / SessionsWeekend 5 · Sessions 9–10 of the year
Book Pages Coveredp. 9 (Q3 بم تعرف ربك, the signs, the creation, the proof-verse)
Slide ReferenceDeck A: Slide 10 (Q3) · Deck B: Lesson 2 (Q3 slides)
Time Needed~45 min per session
MaterialsPrinted packet, pencils, board/whiteboard, a flashlight and a small ball or globe (sun/moon demo, optional)

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 3, students will be able to:

  • Recite Q3 (بِمَ تَعْرِفُ رَبَّكَ) and its answer from memory. (Recall)
  • Sort examples correctly into "signs" (āyāt) vs. "creation" (makhlūqāt). (Comprehension)
  • Recite the proof-verse (Al-A'rāf 7:54) and identify which sūrah and āyah it comes from. (Recall)
  • Explain, in their own words, the difference between a "sign" and a piece of "creation." (Comprehension)
  • Give a new example of a sign or a piece of creation not already listed in the book. (Application)

Weekend 5 · Session 9 (Saturday) — Signs & Creation

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "Look around the room. Name one thing you can see that reminds you Allah exists. Why does it remind you of Him?"

Spiral Review 5 min

Cold-call 3–4 students (mixing Units 1–2): "Man rabbuka?" / "Mā maʿnā ar-Rabb?" — expect instant, full answers.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

[س٣]: فَإِذَا قِيلَ لَكَ: بِمَ تَعْرِفُ رَبَّكَ؟
Question 3: "So if it is said to you: How do you know your Lord?"
[جـ]: فَقُلْ: أَعْرِفُهُ بِآيَاتِهِ وَمَخْلُوقَاتِهِ
Answer: "Then say: I know Him by His signs and His creation."
Teacher script

Explain the difference simply: a sign (āyah) is something that HAPPENS or CHANGES and points to a Planner behind it (like night turning into day). A piece of creation (makhlūq) is a THING that exists because Someone made it (like the sky itself).

Ask: "Could the sun just appear and disappear by accident, every single day, forever, with no one controlling it?" Guide toward: order and repetition this precise point to a Planner, not chance.

مِنْ آيَاتِهِ — His Signs

اللَّيْلُ وَالنَّهَارُ وَالشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ

The night and the day, the sun and the moon

مِنْ مَخْلُوقَاتِهِ — His Creation

السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ وَمَا فِيهِمَا

The heavens and the earth and all that is in them

Exit Ticket 5 min

Have students answer on scrap paper:

1. Is the sun a "sign" or "creation"?

2. Is the sky itself a "sign" or "creation"?

Weekend 5 · Session 10 (Sunday) — The Proof-Verse

Bell Ringer 5 min

Quick recall: "What are the two ways we know our Lord?" (His signs and His creation)

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

Teacher instructions: Every claim in this book is backed by a dalīl (proof) from the Qurʾān or Sunnah — this is the FIRST proof-verse students encounter. Read it slowly, phrase by phrase.

إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ يُغْشِي اللَّيْلَ النَّهَارَ يَطْلُبُهُ حَثِيثًا … تَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ
"Indeed, your Lord is Allah, who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then rose above the Throne. He covers the night with the day, seeking it rapidly … Blessed is Allah, Lord of all the worlds." — Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, 7:54
Teacher script

Point out the "…" — the book itself skips the middle of this āyah and jumps to the ending, marking it with "إِلَى قَوْلِهِ" ("up to His saying…"). Explain: this is a normal, honest way scholars quote part of a longer verse — always show students the full āyah exists even when only part is memorized here.

Ask: "What does 'covers the night with the day' remind you of from earlier — a sign, or creation?" (A sign — it's something that HAPPENS, on repeat, in order.)

Independent Practice 10 min

Sort each item — write "S" for Sign or "C" for Creation:

1. The moon _____    2. The earth _____    3. Daytime turning to night _____    4. The heavens _____

5. What sūrah and āyah number is the proof-verse from?

Partner Activity — Sign or Creation? 10 min

Teacher instructions

Partner A names something from nature (rain, a mountain, sunrise, a river, stars). Partner B must say whether it's a "sign" (something that happens/changes) or "creation" (a thing that exists) and explain why. Switch roles after 5 rounds.

Group Activity — Sign Hunt 10 min

How to play

In small groups, students brainstorm 3 signs and 3 pieces of creation NOT already mentioned in the book (e.g., rain, thunder, a rainbow = signs; mountains, oceans, animals = creation). Each group shares one of each with the class.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize Q3 with its full answer, in Arabic.
  • Tonight, look at the sky. Write down one sign and one piece of creation you noticed.

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. A sign is something that happens or changes over and over in a precise pattern (like night becoming day), while creation is a thing that simply exists because Allah made it (like the sky).

2. Any reasonable example accepted — e.g., rain or a rainbow (sign); a mountain or an animal (creation).

Section F — Recite From Memory

بِمَ تَعْرِفُ رَبَّكَ؟ أَعْرِفُهُ بِآيَاتِهِ وَمَخْلُوقَاتِهِ

Section G — Qurʾān Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

This describes the daily cycle of night and day happening with perfect, unbroken order, over and over, forever. Something that precise and continuous can't happen by pure accident — it points to a Planner and Controller behind it, which is exactly what a "sign" (āyah) is meant to show us.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

I'd tell my friend that understanding HOW something works (the science) doesn't answer WHO set up the system in the first place and keeps it running perfectly every single day. The book teaches that the sun rising precisely on schedule is exactly the kind of order that points to Allah as the Planner behind it — science describes the pattern, but the pattern itself is a sign pointing to Him.
