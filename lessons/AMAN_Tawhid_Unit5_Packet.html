A note for you, the teacher

This is a serious topic for young students — keep the tone matter-of-fact and protective, not frightening. The goal is for students to understand WHY worship belongs to Allah alone, not to leave them anxious. Avoid naming or focusing on real-world examples of specific groups; keep illustrations simple and general (e.g., "asking a rock for help" rather than naming any actual practice or people).

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 5, students will be able to:

  • State the definition of Shirk in their own words. (Comprehension)
  • Name the 5 forms of Shirk the book lists. (Recall)
  • Recite the proof-verse for Shirk's seriousness (Al-Māʾidah 5:72). (Recall)
  • Explain why Shirk is called the greatest sin. (Comprehension)
  • Identify Shirk in a simple example scenario. (Application)

Weekend 9 · Session 17 (Saturday) — What Is Shirk?

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "We learned worship must be for Allah 'alone, with no partner.' What do you think happens if someone directs an act of worship to someone or something else?"

Spiral Review 5 min

Cold-call: "Limā khalaqak?" — expect the full Q4 answer, including "waḥdahu lā sharīka lah."

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

Teacher script

Remind students: the very last words of Q4's answer were "waḥdahu lā sharīka lah" — alone, with no partner. Shirk is exactly the opposite of that: giving a share of worship to something other than Allah.

وَالشِّرْكُ: أَنْ يَجْعَلَ لِلَّهِ نِدًّا
"Shirk is: to set up a rival alongside Allah" (and direct an act of worship to that rival instead of, or alongside, Him).
إِنَّهُ مَنْ يُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ فَقَدْ حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ الْجَنَّةَ وَمَأْوَاهُ النَّارُ وَمَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ مِنْ أَنصَارٍ
"Whoever commits Shirk — Allah has forbidden him Paradise, and his home is the Fire. And the wrongdoers will have no helpers." — Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:72

Teacher script: This is WHY the book calls Shirk the greatest sin — no other sin carries this specific warning about Paradise being forbidden. Keep the tone serious but reassuring: this is why we're careful to learn Tawḥīd so well.

Exit Ticket 5 min

On scrap paper:

In your own words: what is Shirk?

Weekend 9 · Session 18 (Sunday) — The Five Forms of Shirk

Bell Ringer 5 min

Quick recall: "What is Shirk?" — expect a full-sentence answer in students' own words.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

The book lists five specific acts of worship that must be for Allah alone — giving any of them to something else is Shirk:

يَدْعُوهُCalling upon it (duʿāʾ)
يَرْجُوهُHoping in it (rajāʾ)
يَخَافُهُFearing it (khawf)
يَتَوَكَّلُ عَلَيْهِRelying on it (tawakkul)
يَرْغَبُ إِلَيْهِDesiring/turning to it (raghbah)
Teacher script

Go through each with a simple, general example (no naming specific real practices): "If someone asks a statue for help instead of Allah, that's misplacing duʿāʾ. If someone believes a charm will protect them instead of trusting Allah, that's misplacing tawakkul." Keep examples abstract and simple for this age group.

Independent Practice 10 min

1. Name two of the five forms of Shirk listed in the book.

2. Fill in the blank: Shirk is the ______________ sin a person can commit.

Group Activity — True Tawḥīd Circle 10 min

How to play

Sitting in a circle, the teacher names one of the five forms (duʿāʾ, rajāʾ, khawf, tawakkul, raghbah). Going around the circle, each student must say a complete sentence: "I [that action] Allah alone" (e.g., "I call upon Allah alone," "I put my trust in Allah alone"). This reinforces the positive form of Tawḥīd, not just the warning against Shirk.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize the definition of Shirk and the Al-Māʾidah 5:72 proof-verse.
  • Memorize the five forms of Shirk in order.

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. Because the Qurʾān specifically warns that whoever commits it has Paradise forbidden to them and the Fire as their home — no other sin carries that exact warning.

2. It means treating something else as if it deserves the worship that belongs only to Allah — like calling on it, fearing it, or trusting it the way we should only do with Allah.

Section F — Recite From Memory

وَالشِّرْكُ: أَنْ يَجْعَلَ لِلَّهِ نِدًّا … إِنَّهُ مَنْ يُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ فَقَدْ حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ الْجَنَّةَ وَمَأْوَاهُ النَّارُ

Section G — Qurʾān Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

Adding "no helpers" removes any hope of rescue — it shows the seriousness is complete, with no one able to step in and fix it afterward. This makes the warning even stronger: avoiding Shirk isn't something to take lightly.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

Trusting a charm to keep you safe is a form of tawakkul (reliance) — and that reliance belongs to Allah alone. I'd gently explain that feeling safe is good, but the safety itself comes from Allah, not from an object, so it's better to rely on Allah directly through duʿā rather than an item.
