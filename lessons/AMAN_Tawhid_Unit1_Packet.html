Unit 1

The Book's Opening & Why Tawḥīd Comes First

Teacher Overview

Weeks1–2 (Sessions 1–2 of the year)
Book Pages Coveredpp. 7–8 (Introduction, Basmalah, Purpose Statement, Fiṭrah)
Slide ReferenceDeck A: Cover, Slide 2 (Book's Opening), Slide 3 (Why Tawḥīd First) · Deck B: Slides 1–5, 12
Weekend / SessionsWeekend 1–2 · Sessions 1–4 of the year (AMAN meets Sat + Sun each weekend)
Time Needed~45 min per session (see session-by-session breakdown below)
MaterialsPrinted packet, pencils, board/whiteboard, scissors + glue (Day 2 cut-paste), crayons (coloring page)

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 1, students will be able to:

  • Recite the opening of the book (Istiʿādhah, Basmalah, Ḥamdalah, Ṣalawāt, Ammā baʿd) from memory. (Recall)
  • State, in their own words, why the author teaches Tawḥīd before the Qurʾān. (Comprehension)
  • Name the book's two goals — a complete Muslim and a good Muwaḥḥid — and explain what "good" adds to the second goal. (Comprehension)
  • Explain the Question-and-Answer method the book uses and why the author chose it. (Comprehension)
  • Recite the Fiṭrah ḥadīth and explain what it means for how they were born. (Recall + Comprehension)
  • Apply the Fiṭrah concept to a simple real-life scenario. (Application)

Weekend 1 · Session 1 (Saturday) — Meeting the Book

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board as students enter: "If you could only teach a child ONE thing about Islam before anything else, what would you teach them first? Write 1–2 sentences."

Give students 3 minutes to write, then take 2–3 quick answers. Do not correct — this is to surface prior thinking. Come back to this at the end of Day 1.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

Teacher instructions: Read each Arabic line aloud first, have students repeat it (choral response), then give the meaning.

أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ
"I seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Shaytan."
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
"In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate."
الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ، وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى سَيِّدِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ، وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَأَصْحَابِهِ أَجْمَعِينَ
"All praise is for Allah, Lord of all the worlds, and blessings and peace be upon the leader of the Messengers, and upon his family and companions, all of them."
أَمَّا بَعْدُ
"As for what follows" — a classical phrase (the Prophet ﷺ used it too) that signals: the opening praise is done, now the real content begins.

Discussion prompts:

  • Why do you think the author starts by praising Allah before saying anything about his book?
  • The author calls the Prophet ﷺ "leader of the Messengers." What does a leader do?

The Purpose Statement

فَهَذِهِ رِسَالَةٌ نَافِعَةٌ فِيمَا يَجِبُ عَلَى الإِنْسَانِ أَنْ يُعَلِّمَ الصِّبْيَانَ قَبْلَ تَعْلِيمِهِمُ الْقُرْآنَ، حَتَّى يَصِيرَ مُسْلِمًا كَامِلًا عَلَى فِطْرَةِ الإِسْلَامِ، وَمُوَحِّدًا جَيِّدًا عَلَى طَرِيقَةِ الإِيمَانِ
"This is a beneficial treatise on what a person must teach children before teaching them the Qurʾān — so they become a complete Muslim upon the fiṭrah of Islam, and a GOOD Muwaḥḥid upon the path of true faith."
Teacher script

Ask: "Which comes first in this book — Qurʾān or Tawḥīd?" (Tawḥīd). "Why do you think the author put it in that order?" Guide students toward: you need to know WHO you're worshipping and WHY before you can fully understand what you're reading.

Point out the word جَيِّدًا ("good"). Ask: "Why didn't the author just say 'a Muwaḥḥid'? What does adding the word 'good' tell us he wants?" (Not just saying the words — truly understanding and living it.)

Exit Ticket 5 min

Hand out or have students answer on scrap paper:

1. What are the book's two goals for you? (write both in English)

2. In your own words: why does the Imam teach Tawḥīd before the Qurʾān?

Weekend 1 · Session 2 (Sunday) — The Fiṭrah & the Book's Method

Bell Ringer 5 min

Quick recall (cold-call 3–4 students): "What are the book's two goals, from yesterday?" "What word did the author add before Muwaḥḥid, and why does it matter?"

Guided Mini-Lesson: The Question & Answer Method 10 min

وَرَتَّبْتُهُ عَلَى طَرِيقَةِ سُؤَالٍ وَجَوَابٍ
"And I arranged it upon the method of question and answer."

Teacher script: "This whole book is built as questions and answers, like the ones the angels ask in the grave, and like how the angel Jibrīl once questioned the Prophet ﷺ directly. I will ask — you answer, from memory, every single lesson from now on. That's how this book was designed to be taught."

Guided Mini-Lesson: The Fiṭrah Ḥadīth 15 min

كُلُّ مَوْلُودٍ يُولَدُ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ، فَأَبَوَاهُ يُهَوِّدَانِهِ أَوْ يُنَصِّرَانِهِ أَوْ يُمَجِّسَانِهِ
"Every child is born upon the Fiṭrah. Then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian." — Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, narrated by Abū Hurayrah رضي الله عنه
Teacher script

Explain simply: every single baby — everywhere in the world — is born already knowing, deep inside, that Allah is real. That feeling is called the Fiṭrah. Nobody has to convince a baby that a Creator exists.

Ask: "So if we're all born already believing in Allah, why does the book need to TEACH Tawḥīd at all?" Guide toward: the world around us can pull that natural feeling away if nobody nurtures it — this book protects and rebuilds the Fiṭrah on purpose.

Independent Practice 10 min

Students complete individually, then review as a class:

1. Fill in the blank: Every child is born upon the ______________ (English word).

2. True or False: The book teaches the Qurʾān before Tawḥīd.

3. Write, in your own words, what "a good Muwaḥḥid" means:

Partner Activity — Basmalah Relay 10 min

Teacher instructions

Pair students up. Partner A recites the Istiʿādhah + Basmalah from memory while Partner B checks against the printed text (point out any missed word). Switch roles for the Ḥamdalah + Ṣalawāt + Ammā baʿd.

Then Partner A asks Partner B: "What are the book's two goals?" Partner B answers fully in English, then switches: Partner B asks Partner A to explain the Fiṭrah ḥadīth in their own words.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Practice reciting the full opening (Istiʿādhah → Ammā baʿd) three times out loud at home.
  • Ask a parent or older sibling: "What do you think it means that I was born on the Fiṭrah?" Write their answer in 1–2 sentences.
  • Memorize the Fiṭrah ḥadīth in Arabic for a cold-call check next session.

Weekend 2 · Session 3 (Saturday) — Games & Vocabulary Practice

Group Activity — "Complete the Chain" Game

Time: 15 minutes · Group size: 4–6 students

How to play
  • Write each phrase of the opening (Istiʿādhah, Basmalah, Ḥamdalah, Ṣalawāt, Ammā baʿd) on separate cards, one phrase per card, shuffled out of order.
  • Give each group a shuffled set. First group to correctly reorder the whole chain AND recite it aloud together wins.
  • Round 2: swap in cards for the purpose statement's key phrases and repeat.

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. So a child fully understands and believes in Allah first, which helps them understand the Qurʾān better and become a complete Muslim on the natural fiṭrah of Islam.

2. Because it's possible to say the words of Tawḥīd without truly, deeply believing and understanding them — "good" means genuine, well-rooted Tawḥīd, not just repeating words.

Section F — Recite From Memory

كُلُّ مَوْلُودٍ يُولَدُ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ، فَأَبَوَاهُ يُهَوِّدَانِهِ أَوْ يُنَصِّرَانِهِ أَوْ يُمَجِّسَانِهِ

Grading note: award partial credit (3/5) for the first clause alone (كُلُّ مَوْلُودٍ يُولَدُ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ) if the second clause is missing or has errors.

Section G — Ḥadīth Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

The ḥadīth teaches that parents and environment have a powerful effect on a child — even though every child starts out believing in Allah, what they're taught and raised around can move them away from that natural belief. That's exactly why this book exists: to protect and strengthen the Fiṭrah on purpose, instead of leaving it to chance.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

I'd tell my friend that the Imam specifically wrote this book to teach Tawḥīd BEFORE anything else — even before the Qurʾān — because truly knowing who Allah is comes first. Duʿā is important, but the book says its whole purpose is to help you become a complete Muslim and a GOOD Muwaḥḥid — which means real understanding, not just knowing words. Knowing Allah deeply is the foundation everything else, including duʿā, is built on.

Grading note for Sections E, G, H: use the Written Assignment Rubric in the Master Plan — award full credit for any answer that captures the same idea in the student's own words, not just this exact wording.
