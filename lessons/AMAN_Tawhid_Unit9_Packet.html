The Five Pillars of Islam

Teacher Overview

Weekend / SessionsWeekend 17–18 · Sessions 33–36 of the year
Book Pages Coveredpp. 17–21 (Ḥadīth of Jibrīl, proofs for each pillar, Ṭahārah as worship)
Slide ReferenceDeck A: Slides 20–24 (Five Pillars, Ṭahārah) · Deck B: Lesson 4 (Pillars slides)
Time Needed~45 min per session
MaterialsPrinted packet, pencils, board/whiteboard, 5 large cards (one per pillar) for the physical ordering activity

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 9, students will be able to:

  • Recite the Ḥadīth of Jibrīl's definition of Islam and name all five pillars in order. (Recall)
  • Recite the proof-verse for each pillar. (Recall)
  • Explain why the two Shahādahs are listed as the first pillar. (Comprehension)
  • Explain why Ṭahārah (purification) is connected to worship. (Comprehension)
  • Identify which pillar a described action belongs to. (Application)

Weekend 17 · Session 33 (Saturday) — The Ḥadīth of Jibrīl

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "Can you name any of the five pillars of Islam already, before we start? Write as many as you know."

Spiral Review 5 min

Cold-call: "Mā dīnuk?" → "Dīniya al-Islām." Then: "Name the four parts of Islam's meaning."

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

Teacher instructions: This ḥadīth is one of the most famous in all of Islam — the angel Jibrīl appeared to the Prophet ﷺ in front of the Companions and asked him directly what Islam is.

الْإِسْلَامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ، وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ الْبَيْتَ إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا
"Islam is that you testify there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah, establish the prayer, give zakāh, fast Ramaḍān, and make ḥajj to the House if you are able." — Ḥadīth of Jibrīl, narrated by ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (Bukhārī 4777, Muslim 102)
شَهَادَتَان1. Shahādatān
صَلَاة2. Ṣalāh
زَكَاة3. Zakāh
صِيَام4. Ṣiyām
حَجّ5. Ḥajj
Teacher script

Ask: "Why do you think the two testimonies come FIRST, before any action?" Guide toward: everything else is built on that foundation — you can't pray, give zakāh, fast, or make ḥajj sincerely without first testifying to Tawḥīd and the Prophet's ﷺ message.

Exit Ticket 5 min

On scrap paper:

List the five pillars in order.

Weekend 17 · Session 34 (Sunday) — Proofs for Ṣalāh, Zakāh & Ṣawm

Bell Ringer 5 min

Quick recall: "List the five pillars in order" — cold-call 2–3 students.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ حُنَفَاءَ وَيُقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَذَٰلِكَ دِينُ الْقَيِّمَةِ
"And they were not commanded except to worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion, inclining to truth, and to establish prayer and give zakāh — and that is the correct religion." — Sūrat al-Bayyinah 98:5
Teacher script

Point out this single verse proves BOTH Ṣalāh and Zakāh together. Ask: "Why do you think prayer and charity are so often paired in the Qurʾān?" Guide toward: prayer is worship with the body toward Allah; zakāh is worship with wealth toward His creation — together they cover both directions of a Muslim's duty.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
"O you who believe, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may attain taqwā." — Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:183

Teacher script: Point out fasting isn't new to this ummah — it was prescribed for earlier nations too. Ask: "What is the GOAL of fasting, according to this āyah?" (taqwā — God-consciousness, not just hunger.)

Independent Practice 10 min

1. Which pillar is proven by Al-Bayyinah 98:5 (name both)?

2. According to Al-Baqarah 2:183, what is the goal of fasting?

Partner Activity 10 min

Teacher instructions

Partner A names a pillar; Partner B recites its proof-verse (or as much as they can). Switch after each pillar covered so far.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize the Ḥadīth of Jibrīl and the proof-verses for Ṣalāh/Zakāh and Ṣawm.

Weekend 18 · Session 35 (Saturday) — Ḥajj & Ṭahārah as Worship

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "Before you pray, what do you have to do first?" (wuḍūʾ / be in a state of purity)

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا
"And due to Allah from the people is pilgrimage to the House — for whoever is able to find a way there." — Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:97
Teacher script

Point out the condition: "for whoever is able." Ask: "Why do you think Ḥajj — unlike the other pillars — has this specific condition attached?" Guide toward: Ḥajj requires travel, money, and physical ability, which not everyone has; Allah doesn't obligate what a person cannot do.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ
"Indeed, Allah loves those who repent, and He loves those who purify themselves." — Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:222

Teacher script: Explain that Ṭahārah (purification) isn't just a "chore" before prayer — the āyah says Allah LOVES those who purify themselves. This connects physical cleanliness to something Allah is pleased with, making wuḍūʾ itself a form of worship, not just a technical requirement.

Independent Practice 10 min

1. What condition does Āl ʿImrān 3:97 attach to Ḥajj?

2. According to Al-Baqarah 2:222, who does Allah love?

Group Activity — Human Pillars 10 min

How to play

Give 5 students each a large card with one pillar's name. Shuffle them and have them stand in a random line; the rest of the class must direct them (without touching) into the correct order, then the whole class recites the ḥadīth together as each "pillar" raises their card at the right moment.

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize the Ḥajj proof-verse and the Ṭahārah āyah.

Parent signature:

Grading note: award 1 pt per correctly-recalled pillar (in the ḥadīth's phrasing) if the full ḥadīth isn't complete.

Section G — Ḥadīth Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

It shows that belief always comes first and must lead to action — the Shahādahs open the ḥadīth, then everything after flows from that foundation: worship with the body, with wealth, and with self-restraint. Islam isn't belief alone or action alone; it's belief that produces real action across every part of a person's life.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

I'd tell my friend that the Ḥadīth of Jibrīl lists all five as parts of the SAME definition of Islam — not the Shahādah plus four optional extras. Saying the Shahādah is the essential foundation, but the ḥadīth makes clear that establishing prayer, giving zakāh, fasting, and Ḥajj (if able) are just as much a part of what Islam actually is.
