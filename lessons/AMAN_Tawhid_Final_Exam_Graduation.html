Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. So a child understands and believes in Allah first, helping them understand the Qurʾān and become a complete Muslim and good Muwaḥḥid.

2. Allah truly owns and sustains everything that exists, and only the true Owner deserves to be worshipped and obeyed by His creation.

3. The Qurʾān specifically warns Paradise is forbidden and the Fire is the home of whoever commits it — no other sin carries that exact warning.

4. True belief in Allah requires actively rejecting everything that competes with Him first — you can't fully believe in Allah while holding loyalty to something false.

5. Al-Baqarah 2:222 says Allah LOVES those who purify themselves — that makes it something Allah is pleased with, not just a technical requirement.

6. Islam's pillars are mostly actions (praying, fasting); Īmān's pillars are things believed (in Allah, angels, the Last Day).

Section G — Comprehension (sample full-credit answers)

1. "Except" rules out every other possible reason — worship isn't one purpose among many, it's THE purpose behind our existence.

2. Worship isn't about which action but WHO it's directed to — e.g., tawakkul placed in Allah is worship; that same reliance placed in an object is Shirk, since only Allah deserves it.

3. Tawḥīd has always been the one consistent message throughout human history — every nation received a messenger with this exact call, not something new.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answers)

1. Real Islam needs all four parts — belief (Istislām) alone is missing Inqiyād (obedient action), Muwālāh, and Muʿādāh, which show up in how you live, not just what you privately believe.

2. Trusting the charm misplaces tawakkul, which belongs to Allah alone — better to make duʿā and rely on Him directly.

3. The most important thing is learning who Allah truly is and making sure every part of worship belongs to Him alone — everything else in the book builds on that one foundation.

Grading note for Sections E, G, H: use the Written Assignment Rubric — award full credit for the same idea in the student's own words.

Weekend 29 · Session 58 (Sunday) — Oral Recitation Exam

Test each student individually (or in small groups while others review quietly). Score using the Oral Recitation Rubric from the Master Plan.

Required Recitations

1
All six grave-questions with complete answers (Q1–Q6)
2
The Fiṭrah ḥadīth
3
The definition of Shirk and its five forms
4
The Ḥadīth of Jibrīl (Five Pillars)
5
The Six Pillars of Īmān
6
One proof-verse of the student's choice, with its meaning explained
Scoring

Score each of the 6 items out of 4 (using the Oral Recitation Rubric's 4 criteria, averaged), for a maximum of 24 points. Combine with the written exam score for the student's final combined score, or report separately per your madrasah's preference.

Weekend 30 · Session 59 (Saturday) — Results & Reflection

Return Exams & Review Together 25 min

Teacher instructions

Go through the exam as a class, question by question, letting students self-correct in a different color pen. This is a genuine learning opportunity, not just a grading formality — the goal is retention going forward, not just a final score.

Whole-Year Reflection 15 min

Discussion prompts
  • "What was the hardest thing to memorize this year? What helped you learn it?"
  • "What's one thing you understand about Allah now that you didn't at the start of the year?"
  • "How will you keep practicing the six grave-questions after this class ends?"

Graduation Prep 5 min

Assign any closing recitation roles for tomorrow's ceremony (e.g., a student to lead the opening duʿā, another to recite Q1–Q6 for the group).
