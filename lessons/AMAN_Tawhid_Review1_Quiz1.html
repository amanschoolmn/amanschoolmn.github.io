Cumulative Review & Quiz 1

Covering Units 1–3 — The First Checkpoint

Teacher Overview

Weekend / SessionsWeekend 6 · Sessions 11–12 of the year
Content ReviewedALL of Units 1–3: the book's opening, the Fiṭrah, Q1, Q2, meaning of Allah, Q3, signs vs. creation, the proof-verse
PurposeThis is the first spiral-review checkpoint. Nothing here is new content — the goal is retention. If students struggle with an item, reteach it briefly before Quiz 1.
Time Needed~45 min per session
MaterialsPrinted packet, station cards (can print station boxes below as separate cards), pencils
Why this matters This is the pattern for the whole year: every 5–6 sessions, students stop learning new material and instead revisit everything so far. Research on memorization consistently shows spaced repetition beats cramming — treat this session as seriously as a "new content" session, not as a throwaway day.

Weekend 6 · Session 11 (Saturday) — Review Stations

Set up 4 stations around the room (or run them whole-class in sequence if space is limited). Groups rotate every 6–7 minutes. A bell or timer works well to signal rotation.

1

The Opening & the Fiṭrah

Recite together: Istiʿādhah → Basmalah → Ḥamdalah → Ṣalawāt → Ammā baʿd. Then cold-call: "What are the book's two goals?" and "Recite the Fiṭrah ḥadīth."

2

Q1 & Q2 Drill

Flashcard drill in pairs: "Man rabbuka?" → "Rabbī Allāh." "Mā maʿnā ar-Rabb?" → "Al-Mālik al-Maʿbūd." Then: "What does the Name Allah mean?"

3

Signs or Creation?

Call out items rapid-fire; students respond "sign!" or "creation!" as a class: the sun (sign) · the earth (creation) · sunrise (sign) · the sky (creation) · the moon (sign).

4

Vocabulary Speed Round

Show a vocabulary card (Arabic word only); first student to correctly give the English meaning scores a point for their team: al-Fiṭrah, Muwaḥḥid, ar-Rabb, al-Mālik, al-Maʿbūd, āyah, makhlūq.

Whole-Class Game — "Around the World" 10 min

How to play

One student stands next to a seated classmate. Teacher asks any Q&A from Units 1–3 (Q1, Q2, or Q3, or "recite the Fiṭrah ḥadīth"). Whoever answers first moves on to challenge the next seated student. See how far a student can get "around the room."

Weekend 6 · Session 12 (Sunday) — Quiz 1

Warm-Up 10 min

Silent review time: Give students 10 quiet minutes with their Unit 1–3 packets to self-review before the quiz. Circulate and answer questions one-on-one.

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. So a child fully understands and believes in Allah first, which helps them understand the Qurʾān and become a complete Muslim and good Muwaḥḥid.

2. Q1 asks WHO your Lord is (Allah); Q2 asks WHAT the word "Rabb" means (the Owner, the One Worshipped).

3. A sign is something that happens or repeats (like sunrise); creation is a thing that exists because Allah made it (like the sky).

Section F — Recite From Memory

كُلُّ مَوْلُودٍ يُولَدُ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ … مَنْ رَبُّكَ؟ رَبِّي اللَّهُ … مَا مَعْنَى الرَّبِّ؟ الْمَالِكُ الْمَعْبُودُ … بِمَ تَعْرِفُ رَبَّكَ؟ أَعْرِفُهُ بِآيَاتِهِ وَمَخْلُوقَاتِهِ

Grading note: award partial credit per line independently (up to 3 pts each) — a student may recall some Q&As perfectly and others only partially.

Section G — Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

Both show that Allah designed things with intention and order, not by accident — the sun and moon follow a precise, unbroken pattern that couldn't happen randomly, and even something as personal as a child's Fiṭrah follows a real, dependable design (born believing in Allah) that outside influence can only change, not erase.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

I'd tell them: we know Allah is real by His signs and His creation — like how the sun and moon move in perfect order every single day, and how the sky and earth exist because Someone had to make them. Nothing that precise and huge happens by accident.

Grading note for Sections E, G, H: use the Written Assignment Rubric — award full credit for the same idea in the student's own words.
