Weekend 11 · Session 21 (Saturday) — Review Stations

4 stations, rotate every 6–7 minutes.

1

Q4 & ʿIbādah Drill

Cold-call: "Limā khalaqak?" → full Q4 answer. Then: "Name the three categories of ʿIbādah" (statements, outward actions, inward actions).

2

Shirk & the Five Forms

Recite the definition of Shirk together. Then rapid-fire: name the five forms (duʿāʾ, rajāʾ, khawf, tawakkul, raghbah).

3

Duʿā Review

Complete the promise: "Udʿūnī…" → "astajib lakum." Then: "What are the five acts of the heart, and who do they belong to?"

4

Spiral Back — Units 1–3

Mix in older material so it doesn't fade: "What are the book's two goals?" / "Man rabbuka?" / "Bimā taʿrifu rabbaka?"

Whole-Class Game — Shirk or Not Shirk? 15 min

How to play

Read simple scenarios; students give a thumbs-up (fine) or thumbs-down (Shirk): "Asking Allah for help" (fine) · "Asking a rock for help" (Shirk) · "Fearing a lion" (fine, normal fear) · "Believing a charm protects you instead of Allah" (Shirk) · "Trusting a doctor's medicine while trusting Allah for the cure" (fine).

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. Because the Qurʾān specifically warns that Paradise is forbidden and the Fire is the home of whoever commits it — no other sin carries that exact warning.

2. Because refusing to ask Allah for help is really saying you don't need Him, which is the opposite of the humility worship requires.

Grading note: award partial credit per line independently (up to 3 pts each).

Section G — Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

Worship isn't about which specific action you do — it's about WHO you direct it to. Fear, hope, reliance, and calling upon are all meant exclusively for Allah, since He alone truly deserves them; giving that same trust or fear to something else takes something that belongs to Allah and misplaces it, which is exactly what makes it the greatest sin instead of an act of worship.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

Trusting the charm to protect them is misplacing tawakkul (reliance), which the book teaches must go to Allah alone. I'd gently explain that feeling safe is good, but true safety comes from Allah — so instead of the charm, they could make duʿā and trust Allah directly for protection.

Grading note for Sections E, G, H: use the Written Assignment Rubric — award full credit for the same idea in the student's own words.
