A note for you, the teacher

This unit closes out the first third of the year — after Weekend 12 comes the Midterm. Ṭāghūt connects directly back to Shirk (Unit 5): it names the "other side" of what a person must reject in order for their Tawḥīd to be complete.

Learning Objectives

By the end of Unit 7, students will be able to:

  • State the first thing Allah obligated upon His servants. (Recall)
  • Recite An-Naḥl 16:36 and explain what it commands. (Recall + Comprehension)
  • List the five things the book names as examples of Ṭāghūt. (Recall)
  • Recite Ibn al-Qayyim's definition of Ṭāghūt and explain it in their own words. (Comprehension)
  • Explain why belief in Allah must be paired with REJECTING Ṭāghūt, not just believing in Allah alone. (Application)

Weekend 12 · Session 23 (Saturday) — The First Obligation

Bell Ringer 5 min

Write on the board: "We've learned a lot about believing in Allah. Do you think it's enough to just believe in Allah — or is something else needed too?"

Spiral Review 5 min

Cold-call: "What is Shirk?" — expect full-sentence answer from Unit 5.

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

وَأَوَّلُ مَا فَرَضَ اللهُ عَلَى عِبَادِهِ: الْكُفْرُ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَالْإِيمَانُ بِاللهِ
"The first thing Allah obligated upon His servants: disbelief in Ṭāghūt and belief in Allah."
Teacher script

Ask: "Notice the order — which comes FIRST in this sentence, disbelief in Ṭāghūt, or belief in Allah?" (disbelief in Ṭāghūt is named first). Explain: this teaches that TRUE belief in Allah requires actively REJECTING everything false that competes with Him — it's not enough to just quietly believe in Allah while still giving some loyalty to something else.

وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ
"And We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying]: Worship Allah and avoid Ṭāghūt." — Sūrat an-Naḥl 16:36

Teacher script: Point out this verse says EVERY messenger, to EVERY nation, taught this exact same message — Tawḥīd has always been one consistent call throughout history, not something new.

Exit Ticket 5 min

On scrap paper:

What is the first thing Allah obligated upon His servants?

Weekend 12 · Session 24 (Sunday) — What Is Ṭāghūt?

Bell Ringer 5 min

Quick recall: "Worship Allah and avoid ______." (aṭ-Ṭāghūt)

Guided Classroom Activity 20 min

The book names five examples of Ṭāghūt:

1
Whatever is worshipped besides Allah (مَا عُبِدَ مِنْ دُونِ اللهِ)
2
Shayṭān (الشَّيْطَان)
3
A soothsayer (الْكِهَانَة)
4
An astrologer (مُنَجِّم)
5
Whoever rules by other than what Allah revealed, and every followed one obeyed upon falsehood (مَنْ يَحْكُمُ بِغَيْرِ مَا أَنْزَلَ اللهُ)

Ibn al-Qayyim's Definition

الطَّاغُوتُ: مَا تَجَاوَزَ بِهِ الْعَبْدُ حَدَّهُ مِنْ مَعْبُودٍ، أَوْ مَتْبُوعٍ، أَوْ مُطَاعٍ
"Ṭāghūt is whatever a person goes beyond their proper limit with — whether it's worshipped, followed, or obeyed."
— Al-ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (raḥimahullāh)
Teacher script

Explain simply: this definition is broader than just "false gods" — it covers going too far with ANYONE or ANYTHING, even something normally good, like obeying a person's every word even when they command disobedience to Allah. The key word is "beyond the limit" (تَجَاوَزَ حَدَّهُ) — Allah alone has no limit on obedience; everyone else does.

Independent Practice 10 min

1. Name two of the five examples of Ṭāghūt the book lists.

2. Fill in the blank: The first obligation is disbelief in ______________ and belief in ______________.

Partner Activity 10 min

Teacher instructions

Partner A recites An-Naḥl 16:36; Partner B recites Ibn al-Qayyim's definition. Switch and repeat. Then discuss together: "Why do you think 'obeyed' and 'followed' are included, not just 'worshipped'?"

Homework

Take home and return next session:
  • Memorize the first-obligation statement, An-Naḥl 16:36, and Ibn al-Qayyim's definition.
  • This unit closes Weekend 12 — the Midterm Review begins next weekend. Look back over Units 1–7 at home before then.

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. Because true belief in Allah requires actively rejecting everything that competes with Him first — you can't fully believe in Allah while still holding onto loyalty to something false.

2. It means anything a person gives too much devotion to — whether worshipping it, following it, or obeying it — beyond the proper limit, especially when it competes with what's owed to Allah.

Section F — Recite From Memory

وَأَوَّلُ مَا فَرَضَ اللهُ عَلَى عِبَادِهِ: الْكُفْرُ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَالْإِيمَانُ بِاللهِ … وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ

Section G — Qurʾān Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

It tells us Tawḥīd has always been the same single message throughout all of human history — every nation, in every time period, received a messenger with this exact call. It's not a new teaching or something that changed over time; it's the one consistent purpose behind every messenger Allah ever sent.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

If someone obeys a friend or leader even when that person commands something that disobeys Allah — going along with it no matter what, without limit — that obedience has gone "beyond the proper limit," which is exactly what Ibn al-Qayyim's definition describes. Normal respect and following good instructions is fine; the problem is obeying without limit, even into disobedience to Allah.
