Weekend 19 · Session 37 (Saturday) — Review Stations

5 stations, rotate every 5–6 minutes.

1

Q5 & Islam's Meaning

Cold-call: "Mā dīnuk?" → "Dīniya al-Islām." Name the four parts (Istislām, Inqiyād, Muwālāh, Muʿādāh).

2

Lā ilāha illā Allāh

"What does Lā ilāha illā Allāh mean, word for word?" Recite Ibrāhīm's declaration.

3

The Five Pillars

List all five pillars in order from the Ḥadīth of Jibrīl. Recite one proof-verse of choice.

4

Ṭahārah

"Who does Allah love, per Al-Baqarah 2:222?" Recite the āyah.

5

Full Spiral — Units 1–7

Mix freely: Q1–Q4, the Fiṭrah ḥadīth, the definition of Shirk, the five acts of the heart, the first obligation regarding Ṭāghūt.

Weekend 19 · Session 38 (Sunday) — Quiz 3

Warm-up: 10 quiet minutes of self-review with Unit 8–9 packets before the quiz.

Section G — Comprehension (4 pts)

Section E — Short Answer (sample full-credit answers)

1. Because everything else is built on that foundation — sincere prayer, zakāh, fasting, and ḥajj all depend on first testifying to Tawḥīd and the Prophet's ﷺ message.

2. Because real Islam is a complete way of life that shows up in relationships too, not just private belief — standing with fellow Muslims and rejecting Shirk are both part of living out Tawḥīd.

Grading note: award partial credit per line independently (up to 3 pts each).

Section G — Comprehension (sample full-credit answer)

Both teach that belief must come first and lead to action — the Shahādahs open the ḥadīth just as Istislām comes before Inqiyād in Islam's meaning. Believing without acting on it is incomplete in both lessons; true Islam requires both together.

Section H — Scenario (sample full-credit answer)

I'd remind my classmate that Al-Baqarah 2:222 says Allah LOVES those who purify themselves — that makes Ṭahārah an act of worship Allah is pleased with, not just a formality to check off before prayer. Taking it seriously is itself part of worship.

Grading note for Sections E, G, H: use the Written Assignment Rubric — award full credit for the same idea in the student's own words.
